# Unified strategic controller

The current game runs one controller for all AI factions and player handover: Strategic command. The previous passive/aggressive/retaliatory/opportunistic/volatile personalities and difficulty timing variants are disabled. Their old save identifiers remain accepted solely for migration to the unified controller. This is a deterministic strategy implementation, not a claim to reproduce native DEFCON AI or real politics.

## Why the previous AI was ineffective

The old controller selected one launcher, limited its team to 1–3 in-flight warheads, paused after every launch, and switched each silo back to defense after one shot. This trickle was easy for the current 10-degree, 8-second-reload SAM network to intercept. AI also left deployable stock unused, reducing its forces before combat began.

## Current operation

- **Deployment:** at DEFCON 5–4, one placement every 30 game seconds uses actual remaining stock. Sites fit real territory/land geometry, retain 4-degree installation spacing, and complete six-ship fleets fit maritime zones and coastline margins. Fleet centers are separated; a denser candidate search handles the narrow West Asian maritime region. The player retains manual deployment unless handing over control. Enemy placements do not expose hidden locations through messages.
- **Reconnaissance and focus:** target utility uses public cities and actually detected/remembered installations. Detected aggression, target value, distance and continuity choose a focus opponent. Undiscovered installations and hidden missile destinations are not intelligence inputs. Full view remains display-only.
- **Force movement:** fleets advance toward viable water stations near-shore stations that bring submarines inside their 3-degree weapon radius, giving submarine missiles usable launch positions. Visible local enemy/own naval strength determines attack versus retreat. Fighters scout/patrol; naval bombers and ASW aircraft retain their real host inventory and fuel limits.
- **Strike packages:** after an initial 60-second DEFCON 1 staging period (10 seconds in Lab), the planner selects a target, estimates the known defending batteries and required impacts, and assigns a package up to 72 warheads. Unknown coverage uses a conservative two-battery estimate. Actual eligible launchers, payloads, bomber fuel and a 6% arsenal reserve bound the package; retaliation lowers the reserve to 2%. Detectable incoming traffic leaves part of the silo force defending. Small remaining arsenals use only their actual available sources rather than inventing extra missiles.
- **Coordination:** silos, submarines and bomber hosts receive persisted FIFO commands with absolute notBefore times. A common impact deadline accounts for travel distance, the real 0.06-degree/s warhead speed, bomber ingress, preparation and current cooldown. Subsequent rounds remain 120 game seconds apart per launcher. Commands fire through the normal gameplay launch methods; there is no teleport, instant impact, extra ammunition or bypassed reload. Surfaced missile fleets hold their station during their scheduled launches.
- **Continuous pressure:** active packages are tracked independently. Free platforms may prepare follow-up packages after 30 seconds, or 10 seconds under attack, while older missiles are still in flight. Follow-ups use at least three available platforms (two for counterstrikes, or the previous force size when smaller). Outstanding orders and airborne bomber payloads are deducted before reserving more ammunition. Targets with sufficient committed strikes are skipped in favor of other threats. Destroyed or newly allied queued targets cancel through the existing order rules. Silo sources return to defense after their assigned package is launched even if they retain ammunition. Target utility avoids unnecessary repeat strikes when enough friendly weapons are already committed.
- **Reaction:** tactical planning runs every 10 game seconds for every faction, with 10-second strategic reviews. Ground intelligence memory refreshes every 5 seconds; live rendering, detection and weapon locks still use current positions. This avoids rescanning every undiscovered installation for every frame once all ten factions deploy their full forces.

## Control and persistence

Hand over to AI / Take command continue to work. Accepted manual queues are not redirected by the planner; manual unit overrides remain protected. A takeover keeps accepted orders. Scheduled launches, target references, wave IDs, RNG, current packages and next review times survive server checkpoints. Old personality saves migrate once to strategy version 3 with a short preparation window, preserving simulation progress, ammo, cities and manual orders. Legacy difficulty values are read for compatibility but do not select different AI behavior. Settings exposes only Strategic command.

Battleship range is now 3 degrees, superseding the earlier 4-degree close-engagement profile. The other requested short-range weapons, slow warheads and fast SAM rules remain unchanged.

Detected incoming missiles identify an aggressor for target prioritization and shorten counterstrike staging to 10 seconds, even before any impact. Hidden missile destinations still do not supply intelligence, and a successful interception does not count as a nuclear impact.


## Offensive-defense and short-range submarine update

The unified controller now reacts to detected conventional incursions, actual damage and observed warhead engagements. SAM engagement records the detected aggressor before the missile can disappear through interception; this avoids missing an attack between ten-second planning reviews. Counterattack priority lasts 180 game seconds and favors the recent aggressor over an older rival, without claiming a nuclear impact occurred. Nuclear impacts still record actual retaliation state. Nuclear authorization remains locked until DEFCON 1.

Submarine torpedo and nuclear weapon radii are both 3 degrees; passive/active/ASW detection ranges remain separate. AI submarines prepare active torpedo combat against detected nearby ships before nuclear authorization. Missile fleets seek reachable coastal firing stations instead of waiting 8–16 degrees offshore. Group centers stay about 3.4–3.8 degrees away so a forward submarine is within 3 degrees while the whole fleet remains outside the 2-degree nuclear damage footprint. A compact circular formation can rotate to face an armed submarine toward shore. This changes physical group waypoints, preserves the 1-degree circle and 1-degree coastline margin, and does not teleport or detach ships. Rotation is committed only after the route and all endpoints validate.

Under local air/naval pressure, hosts deploy up to five real aircraft instead of limiting combat to two or three. Nearby detected warheads take interception priority; a missile attacking another region does not automatically turn every remote carrier away from offense. Fleets replan every 90 game seconds, engage through moderate numerical disadvantage, and use a short local withdrawal when heavily outmatched rather than immediately returning all the way home.

Revision-2 AI saves preserve their existing accepted wave schedules when upgraded. Multiple active packages, aggressor memory, rotated fleet orientation and manual controls all persist. No personality/difficulty variants were reintroduced.

### Movement/durability revision 6

Bomber arrival estimates now receive `TYPES.bomber.speed` (0.25) instead of a hard-coded 0.05. Hidden known-installation estimates use revised HP (silo 40, airbase 30, radar 6). Nuclear submarine firing stations use the separate 20° nuclear range; torpedo engagement remains 3°. The real forward-airbase benchmark still passes: seed 42 produces 58 launches, 10 detonations, one defender lost and three overlapping packages; seed 91 produces 56 launches, 18 detonations, three defenders lost and five overlapping packages. These results reflect the new HP, hit chances and movement, so should not be compared directly with earlier balance revisions. Automated suite: 207 passing tests.

### Combat revision 7 deployment

All AI reserves now deploy at the first ten-second review. Cached plans generated by `scripts/plan-deployments.mjs` cover all ten territories and include coastal-city candidates on multiple sea fronts. Population coverage and installation spacing drive land placement; separated legal centers drive fleets. Runtime `sim.deploy` remains the final land/sea/stock validator, and a dynamic fallback handles legacy/custom maps. On the local thirty-second fixture, all AI stock was exhausted legally; total units were 496 and maximum tick was about 33ms. Player stock stayed unchanged.
