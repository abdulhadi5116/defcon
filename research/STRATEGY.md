# Unified strategic controller

The current game runs one controller for all AI factions and player handover: Strategic command. The previous passive/aggressive/retaliatory/opportunistic/volatile personalities and difficulty timing variants are disabled. Their old save identifiers remain accepted solely for migration to the unified controller. This is a deterministic strategy implementation, not a claim to reproduce native DEFCON AI or real politics.

## Why the previous AI was ineffective

The old controller selected one launcher, limited its team to 1–3 in-flight warheads, paused after every launch, and switched each silo back to defense after one shot. This trickle was easy for the current 10-degree, 8-second-reload SAM network to intercept. AI also left deployable stock unused, reducing its forces before combat began.

## Current operation

- **Deployment:** at DEFCON 5–4, one placement every 30 game seconds uses actual remaining stock. Sites fit real territory/land geometry, retain 4-degree installation spacing, and complete six-ship fleets fit maritime zones and coastline margins. Fleet centers are separated; a denser candidate search handles the narrow West Asian maritime region. The player retains manual deployment unless handing over control. Enemy placements do not expose hidden locations through messages.
- **Reconnaissance and focus:** target utility uses public cities and actually detected/remembered installations. Detected aggression, target value, distance and continuity choose a focus opponent. Undiscovered installations and hidden missile destinations are not intelligence inputs. Full view remains display-only.
- **Force movement:** fleets advance toward viable water stations 8–16 degrees from a rival's coastal population, giving submarine missiles usable launch positions. Visible local enemy/own naval strength determines attack versus retreat. Fighters scout/patrol; naval bombers and ASW aircraft retain their real host inventory and fuel limits.
- **Strike packages:** after an initial 180-second DEFCON 1 staging period (30 seconds in Lab), the planner selects a target, estimates the known defending batteries and required impacts, and assigns a package up to 48 warheads. Unknown coverage uses a conservative two-battery estimate. Actual eligible launchers, payloads, bomber fuel and a 12% arsenal reserve bound the package; retaliation lowers the reserve to 6%. Detectable incoming traffic leaves part of the silo force defending. Small remaining arsenals use only their actual available sources rather than inventing extra missiles.
- **Coordination:** silos, submarines and bomber hosts receive persisted FIFO commands with absolute notBefore times. A common impact deadline accounts for travel distance, the real 0.06-degree/s warhead speed, bomber ingress, preparation and current cooldown. Subsequent rounds remain 120 game seconds apart per launcher. Commands fire through the normal gameplay launch methods; there is no teleport, instant impact, extra ammunition or bypassed reload. Surfaced missile fleets hold their station during their scheduled launches.
- **Assessment:** new packages wait for the current package's pending commands, sorties and in-flight missiles to resolve, then reassess surviving targets after 60 seconds. Destroyed or newly allied queued targets cancel through the existing order rules. Silo sources return to defense after their assigned package is launched even if they retain ammunition. Target utility avoids unnecessary repeat strikes when enough friendly weapons are already committed.
- **Reaction:** tactical planning runs every 10 game seconds for every faction, with 30-second strategic reviews. Ground intelligence memory refreshes every 5 seconds; live rendering, detection and weapon locks still use current positions. This avoids rescanning every undiscovered installation for every frame once all ten factions deploy their full forces.

## Control and persistence

Hand over to AI / Take command continue to work. Accepted manual queues are not redirected by the planner; manual unit overrides remain protected. A takeover keeps accepted orders. Scheduled launches, target references, wave IDs, RNG, current packages and next review times survive server checkpoints. Old personality saves migrate once to strategy version 2 with a short preparation window, preserving simulation progress, ammo, cities and manual orders. Legacy difficulty values are read for compatibility but do not select different AI behavior. Settings exposes only Strategic command.

Battleship range is now 3 degrees, superseding the earlier 4-degree close-engagement profile. The other requested short-range weapons, slow warheads and fast SAM rules remain unchanged.

## Verification

`node qa/unified-ai-simulation.mjs` runs the actual engine against three prepared defensive silos, with six attacking silos and four real airfields. For seeds 42 and 91, the first package assigns 19 warheads across ten platforms; 7 and 6 projected first-round impacts converge within 15 seconds. Over 1,800 game seconds, each attacker actually launches 25 nuclear payloads over two packages. The attacks produce 9 / 4 detonations, destroy one defensive installation in each run and retain 72 / 69 nuclear payloads. Interception, aircraft losses, damage, cooldown and fuel are active. These fixed scenarios establish coordinated penetration, not a universal win-rate claim.

184 tests cover real deployment and coast geometry, narrow-zone fleet placement, no early launch, finite cooldowns, reserve limits, hidden intelligence, synchronized arrival forecasts, exact combat continuation after save/reload, legacy profile migration and protected manual control. Browser checks cover migration in the same session, visible multi-order launch queues, a single Settings controller, reload, handover/takeover, placement and mobile controls. Safari remains untested.
